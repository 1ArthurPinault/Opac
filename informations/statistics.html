Statistics

The numbers behind
why Opac exists

Hiring bias is not a feeling, it is a measured, repeated finding across independent studies. Here is the evidence, organized by category, with every source named.

French hiring discrimination based on perceived origin has been documented for over a decade through matched-CV field experiments.

25+

Criteria on which hiring discrimination is explicitly prohibited under French law, from origin to religion and political opinions.

Source: Code du travail, article L1132-1

+20%

Higher rejection rate for candidates with a Maghreb-sounding name compared to equally qualified candidates with a French-sounding name.

Source: DARES, IPP, ISM Corum, testing 2021

18% vs 25%

Interview callback rate for an administrative-role candidate with a Maghreb-sounding name, versus an equivalent reference candidate, in Ile-de-France.

Source: CNRS testing campaign, 2021-2022

~40%

Average hiring discrimination gap measured against candidates from an immigration background, based on matched-CV field experiments in the Paris region.

Source: INSEE, Economie et Statistique n.464-465-466, 2013

Gender pay and access gaps persist in France even where a role and employer are identical.

22.2%

Average gap between women's and men's overall annual salary income in the French private sector, all working time included.

Source: INSEE Focus n.349, March 2025 (2023 data)

14.2%

The gap that remains between women and men at equivalent full-time working hours, once part-time work is factored out.

Source: INSEE Focus n.349, March 2025 (2023 data)

~4%

The gap that remains for the exact same position, at the same employer: the closest available measure to pure pay discrimination.

Source: INSEE, 2023 data, published 2025

24%

Share of women among the best-paid 1% of private-sector positions, despite representing 42% of full-time equivalent jobs.

Source: INSEE Focus n.349, March 2025 (2023 data)

Employment access for people with a recognized disability remains far below the national average, despite a legal quota in place since 1987.

39%

Employment rate for people with a recognized disability in France, nearly half the rate of the overall working-age population.

Source: INSEE data via Observatoire des inegalites, 2023

68%

Employment rate for the overall working-age population in France, shown here for comparison.

Source: INSEE, 2023 data

12%

Unemployment rate among people with a recognized disability, close to double the 7% rate measured for the total workforce.

Source: INSEE data via Observatoire des inegalites, 2023

28%

Share of companies subject to France's disability-employment quota that still employ no disabled worker at all.

Source: Agefiph, national tableau de bord, 2025

Workplace discrimination against LGBTQ+ employees in France has increased in the most recent barometer, even as visibility has grown.

37%

Of LGBT+ employees report having experienced discrimination from their own management, up 12 points in two years.

Source: Ifop for L'Autre Cercle, LGBTQIA+ barometer, 2026

72%

Of LGBT+ employees are now visible about their orientation or gender identity at work, up 19 points in five years.

Source: Ifop for L'Autre Cercle, LGBTQIA+ barometer, 2026

65%

Of transgender employees report having experienced at least one act of discrimination within their organization.

Source: Ifop for L'Autre Cercle, LGBTQIA+ barometer, 2026

2x

Fewer discriminations reported inside companies that signed L'Autre Cercle's LGBTQIA+ commitment charter, compared to the national average.

Source: Ifop for L'Autre Cercle, LGBTQIA+ barometer, 2026

Methodology and sources

Every figure on this page comes from a named, publicly available study or official statistical source. Where a study offers several ways to measure the same gap, we show the most relevant ones rather than picking only the largest number.
